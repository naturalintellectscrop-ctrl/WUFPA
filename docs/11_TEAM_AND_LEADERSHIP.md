# 11 — Team and Leadership

*The people who run WUFPA, how they are presented, and the privacy rules that govern it.*

---

## 1. Why leadership matters disproportionately here

For audience A2 — the funder, the embassy, the regulator — the question behind every other
question is **"who is accountable?"**. An organisation that cannot name the people
responsible for its money and its decisions does not get funded, regardless of how good its
programmes look.

WUFPA answers this unusually well. It has an elected Executive Committee with a stated term,
a Board of Trustees chaired by its own founder, a Disciplinary Committee, a Supervisory
Committee, coordination teams in all six sub-regions, and heads for ten craft guilds `[P5]`–`[P7]`.
That is roughly 60 named office-holders — a genuine governance structure, not a diagram
drawn for a grant application.

**The leadership pages exist to make that visible.** The prototype instead showed four stock
photographs of unrelated people (X1), which converts the organisation's strongest credibility
asset into its largest liability.

Chairperson of the Board of Trustees. Mushana Amos — founding Vice President — is Secretary

a funder is looking for and most small associations cannot show it.

regional role and a guild headship — Tayebwa Agnes is Treasurer and Actors Guild head;

governance and craft structures being genuinely interlocked rather than parallel — and it is
the reason the data model must support one person holding many roles.

## 5. Presentation strategy

### 5.1 Three levels of presentation

Roughly 60 office-holders cannot all be presented the same way without producing a page
nobody reads.

| Level | Who | Treatment | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 — Full profile** | The four documented biographies | Own page: portrait, roles, term, company, biography, related links | `/leadership/[person]/` |
| **2 — Card** | Executive Committee, Board of Trustees | PersonCard: portrait or initials, name, role, district | `/leadership/executive/`, `/leadership/trustees/` |
| **3 — Roster row** | Sub-region teams, guild heads, disciplinary committee | RosterTable: name, role, district | Region pages, guild pages, `/about/governance/` |

**Do not attempt a full portrait grid of 60 people.** Only four portraits exist. A grid of 56
initials-avatars and four photographs looks broken; a roster table looks organised.

### 5.2 Portraits

| Person | Source | Pixels | Usable at |
|---|---|---|---|
| Katabazi George | `[P5]` | 932 × 1122 | Full profile hero, card, roster |
| Cyril Baryabawe | `[P4]` | 793 × 871 | Full profile, card |
| Mushana Amos | `[P4]` | 316 × 409 | Card only — **not** a profile hero |
| Rev. Mwesigwa | `[P4]` | 370 × 434 | Card only — **not** a profile hero |

**Rules:**

- **Never upscale.** Two of the four portraits are small; they are used small
  ([03 § 7.4](03_BRAND_GUIDELINES.md#74-technical-and-ethical-standards)).
- **Never substitute a stock photograph** where no portrait exists (X1). Use the typographic
  initials fallback ([08 § 19](08_COMPONENT_LIBRARY.md#19-personcard)).
- Consistent crop (`--ratio-portrait`, 3:4) and consistent treatment across all four, despite
  their differing origins.
- Alt text: "{Name}, {role}".
- Higher-resolution portraits are content request Q18.

## 6. Privacy and personal data

**This is the most legally significant section in this documentation set.**

### 6.1 What the source contains

Pages 6 and 7 of the profile PDF contain a governance roster with **names, roles, districts
and personal mobile telephone numbers for approximately 60 individuals** `[P6] [P7]`.

### 6.2 The position taken

| Data | Status |
|---|---|
| Names, roles, terms of office, districts, sub-regions | **Publishable** with consent — this is governance information, and publishing it is the point of the page |
| Production company affiliations | **Publishable** with consent |
| Portraits | **Publishable** with explicit consent per person |
| **Personal mobile telephone numbers** | **Not published. Anywhere. Under any circumstances.** |
| **Personal email addresses** | **Not published** |

### 6.3 Why

1. **Uganda's Data Protection and Privacy Act, 2019 applies.** Publishing personal contact
   data of volunteers and office-holders on a public website requires a lawful basis and
   informed consent, and creates an ongoing obligation.
2. **Concrete harm.** Published mobile numbers are harvested within days. The people on this
   roster are volunteers in a creative association; exposing them to fraud, spam and
   harassment is a real cost imposed on them by a website decision they were not consulted on.
3. **It is unnecessary.** Every legitimate need — a member wanting their coordinator, a
   journalist wanting a comment, a funder wanting the Treasurer — is served by routing
   through association channels.
4. **It exposes WUFPA.** The association becomes the data controller for 60 people's personal
   contact details, published without a documented basis.

**These numbers are deliberately not reproduced in this documentation set either.** They
remain in the client's own source document at `[P6] [P7]`, where anyone who needs them can
find them. This repository will live in version control and may become public; personal data
in a git history is effectively permanent.

### 6.4 What is published instead

| Instead of | Publish |
|---|---|
| A coordinator's mobile number | Their name, role and district, plus a contact form that routes to their sub-region |
| The President's mobile number | The association telephone ⟦Q3⟧ |
| Individual email addresses | The association email ⟦Q3⟧ |

> The prototype publishes `+256 701 927 701` as the head office number `[SITE]`. That is the
> number recorded against Katabazi George personally `[P6]` (X7). WUFPA may of course choose
> to publish its President's line — but that must be a decision he consents to, and it must be
> labelled as what it is.

### 6.5 Consent — what is required before launch

Question Q4. Required per person appearing on the site:

1. Consent to publish their **name and role**
2. Consent to publish their **photograph**, where one exists
3. Confirmation of the **correct spelling** of their name (Q1)
4. A record of when consent was given, and a route to withdraw it

For roster-level entries (level 3), an association resolution recorded in AGM or committee
minutes may be sufficient — that is a judgement for WUFPA. For the four full profiles with
photographs, individual written consent is the appropriate standard.

**The site must be built so that a person can be removed on request without breaking
anything** — which follows automatically from modelling people as content entities rather
than hard-coding them into templates ([14 § 5](14_TECHNICAL_ARCHITECTURE.md)).

## 7. Biography guidelines

For writing the four existing biographies to web, and any future ones.

### 7.1 Structure

1. **Role and term** — one line, factual
2. **Their company** — one line
3. **What they did** — the substantive paragraph, specific and dated
4. **What they do now** — current focus

### 7.2 Rules

| Rule | Reason |
|---|---|
| **150–250 words** | Long enough to be substantive, short enough to be read on a phone |
| **Achievements are dated and specific** | "Pioneered the UCC Regional Film Competition" `[P5]` beats "has worked tirelessly" |
| **Name the organisations** | UNESCO, UFMI, ZIFF, Mashariki `[P5]`. Proper nouns carry the credibility ([06 § 6.2](06_WEBSITE_STRATEGY.md#62-specificity-is-the-whole-technique)) |
| **Third person, present tense for current roles** | Standard for institutional biography |
| **Titles as WUFPA uses them** | "Rev.", "Mr" — preserved from the source, pending Q1 |
| **No honorifics of praise** | Not "the visionary Mr X". The source uses "visionary founders" `[P4]`; in WUFPA's own profile that is acceptable, but the website should let the achievements do it |
| **No personal information beyond professional role** | No family, age, or private detail |
| **Never invent, embellish or infer** | Four biographies are sourced. Fifty-six people have no biography, and that is fine — they get a roster row |
| **They/them where pronouns are unknown** | The source uses honorifics for the four documented profiles; for anyone else, do not infer a person's pronouns from their name |

### 7.3 Template for future biographies

> **{Name}** has served as {role} of WUFPA since {year}. {He/She/They} {is/are} {position} of
> {company}, based in {district}.
>
> {Substantive paragraph: what they have done, with dates, places and named partners.}
>
> {Current focus paragraph.}

Collecting biographies for the remaining Executive Committee members would meaningfully
strengthen the governance page. Recorded as a content request, not a launch blocker.

## 8. Website ownership after launch

Not a content question, but it belongs with the people who will do it.

The **Publicity and Communications Officer** `[P6]` is the natural owner of the website. The
handover in [18_DEPLOYMENT_AND_MAINTENANCE.md](18_DEPLOYMENT_AND_MAINTENANCE.md) should be
addressed to that portfolio rather than to an individual, so that ownership survives an
election.

Given constraint C4 — editorial capacity is unknown and probably thin — the realistic ask is
small and should be stated as such:

| Task | Frequency | Effort |
|---|---|---|
| Publish a news item | Monthly, or when something happens | 20 minutes |
| Add an event | As scheduled | 10 minutes |
| Add photographs to an album | After each activity | 15 minutes |
| Review that no "upcoming" event has gone stale | Handled automatically ([09 § 9.2](09_PROGRAMMES_AND_EVENTS.md#92-status-handling--the-part-that-matters)) | 0 |
| Update leadership after an election | Five-yearly | 1 hour |

**Design the maintenance burden down to what the organisation can actually carry.** A site
requiring weekly attention from an association with no communications staff will be abandoned
in four months, and an abandoned site is worse for WUFPA's credibility than no site at all.
