# 03 — Brand Guidelines

*How WUFPA looks and sounds. Derived from the official logo and the organisation's own
words — not invented.*

> **Scope.** WUFPA has a logo and a strapline. It does not have a written brand guideline,
> a typeface, a colour specification or a photography policy. This document establishes
> those for digital use, building outward from what genuinely exists so that the website
> and the printed profile look like the same organisation. Everything derived rather than
> supplied is marked as a **recommendation**.

---

## 1. Brand personality

WUFPA's character is not aspirational marketing language. It is legible in the eight-year
activity record: what the organisation repeatedly chooses to do.

| Trait | What it means here | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| **Organised** | WUFPA's instinct is always to structure things — committees, guilds, coordinators, a distributors group, a SACCO. It is a body that turns loose activity into institutions | `[P5] [P6] [P7] [P23]` |
| **Grounded** | The work happens in Kasese, Kagadi, Sheema, Rukungiri — in community halls, on grass, at kingdom offices. Not in conference centres | `[P8]`–`[P11] [P18]` |
| **Insistent** | Marched through Mbarara for copyright. Lobbied a national regulator until it created a competition. Kept sensitising district by district for eight years | `[P2] [P13]` |
| **Collegial** | Founders moved to the Board rather than out. Partners are hosted, welcomed, photographed together. Learning from outside is sought, not resisted | `[P6] [P15]`–`[P17] [P22]` |
| **Ambitious without pretence** | Wants a regional film studio, curriculum reform and international festival presence — and says plainly that it needs cameras, lights and seed capital to get there | `[P3]` |

**The personality in one line:** *serious people doing organised work in places the industry
forgot.*

**Therefore the brand should feel:** confident, specific, plainly spoken, warm towards
members, precise towards institutions.

**Therefore the brand should never feel:** slick, corporate-generic, pitying, self-
congratulatory, or like a template with a logo dropped in.

## 2. Brand voice

### 2.1 The voice in four rules

**1. Evidence, not adjectives.**
WUFPA does not need to say it is credible. It needs to say what it did, where, when, and
with whom — and let the reader conclude.

> ✗ "WUFPA is a leading and highly respected organisation with a strong track record."
> ✓ "In 2022 we signed a memorandum of understanding with Sky TV Mbarara. Members' films
> now air on TV West, Tayari West TV, GNTV, Lite TV and YoTV."

**2. Plain, complete sentences.**
The reader may be reading English as a second or third language, on a small screen, in a
hurry. Short sentences. Ordinary words. No jargon that a filmmaker in Kanungu would have to
look up.

> ✗ "WUFPA leverages synergistic multi-stakeholder frameworks to catalyse regional creative
> ecosystem development."
> ✓ "We bring filmmakers together so that broadcasters, funders and government have someone
> to talk to."

**3. Members are colleagues, not beneficiaries.**
This is the single most important rule and the one most often broken by organisations of
this type. WUFPA members are working professionals who own companies. They are not people
being helped.

> ✗ "We support disadvantaged youth in rural communities to access opportunities in film."
> ✓ "Our members run production companies in every sub-region of Western Uganda. We open
> the doors that a single company cannot open alone."

Note the distinction: WUFPA's own funding case legitimately talks about opening employment
and income opportunities for underrepresented groups `[P3]` — that is a true statement about
outcomes, made to funders. It is not how WUFPA talks *to* or *about* its members.

**4. Name the place.**
"Western Uganda" is the region. But the specific power is in the names: Mbarara, Kabale,
Kasese, Fort Portal, Masindi, Sheema, Rukungiri, Kanungu, Kagadi, Ibanda. Use them. They
tell a member "this includes me" and tell a funder "this is real coverage".

### 2.2 Voice by audience

The voice does not change; the emphasis does.

| Audience | Lead with | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Prospective member (A1) | Belonging, access, what you get, who to talk to near you | Institutional language, acronyms without expansion |
| Institutional partner (A2) | Legal status, governance, dated delivery, named partners | Emotional appeal, unquantified claims |
| Existing member (A3) | Deadlines, opportunities, practical steps | Re-explaining what WUFPA is |
| Press / researcher (A4) | Facts, dates, correct names, contact | Marketing copy |

### 2.3 Person and tense

- **"We"** for WUFPA in its own voice on About, Programmes, Membership. It is a membership
  body; "we" is literally true.
- **"WUFPA"** in third person for metadata, structured data, press material and any context
  where the sentence may be quoted out of context.
- **"You"** for the reader in membership and calls to action.
- **Past tense for what happened. Present for what is ongoing. Future only with a
  confirmed date.** Never describe a planned event in the future tense once its date has
  passed — this is how the Awards Gala `⚠ VERIFY` becomes a credibility problem
  ([02 § 3](02_ORGANISATION_PROFILE.md#3-history)).

## 3. Editorial style

| Element | Rule | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| **Headlines** | Sentence case. Statement, not label. Max ~9 words | "Eight years. Six sub-regions. 300 production houses." works; "Our Impact" says nothing |
| **Standfirst / intro** | One or two sentences, ≤ 40 words, sets the frame | Gives scanners the whole page in one paragraph |
| **Body paragraphs** | 2–4 sentences. One idea each | Mobile readability |
| **Subheadings** | Every 2–3 paragraphs on long pages | Scanability; also the structure screen-reader users navigate by |
| **Lists** | Only for genuinely parallel items. Never a list of full sentences pretending to be bullets | A page of bullets has no argument |
| **Numbers** | Always with their unit and their date. "300+ production houses (2025)" | An undated statistic ages into a lie |
| **Quotes** | Attributed with full name and role, always | Unattributed quotes read as invented — and here they would be |
| **Captions** | Who, what, where, when — in that order, as far as known. Omit unknowns rather than guessing | See [12_MEDIA_LIBRARY.md](12_MEDIA_LIBRARY.md) |
| **Acronyms** | Expand on first use per page: "Uganda Communications Commission (UCC)" | Pages are entered directly from search, so "first use on the site" is not a useful concept |
| **Links** | Descriptive text. Never "click here" or a bare URL | 2.4.4 / 2.4.9 |
| **Emphasis** | Bold sparingly for scanning anchors. Italics for titles of films and publications. Never ALL CAPS for emphasis in body copy | Caps reduce legibility and are read letter-by-letter by some screen readers |

### 3.1 Words to use

organise · train · represent · negotiate · member · production house · sub-region · guild ·
coordinator · workshop · master class · screening · distribution · copyright · register ·
delegation

### 3.2 Words to avoid, and why

**Scope:** this list governs *website copy* — the words a visitor reads. It does not govern
this documentation, where some of these words are unavoidable technical terms. "Visitor
journey" is standard information-architecture vocabulary; "landscape" is an image
orientation; "leverage" as a noun meaning negotiating power is precise. The ban is on
consultancy filler in published copy, not on the words themselves in every context.

| Avoid | Reason |
|---|---|
| "Empower", "empowering" | Appears in WUFPA's own funding language `[P3]` and is fine *there*. In member-facing copy it makes members sound powerless |
| "Beneficiary", "the underprivileged", "grassroots people" | Positions members as recipients (voice rule 3) |
| "Vibrant", "bustling", "hidden gem", "untapped potential" | Tourist-brochure register applied to a place people live |
| "Passionate", "dedicated", "committed" | Unfalsifiable. Every organisation claims them |
| "Journey", "space", "ecosystem", "landscape", "leverage", "unlock", "seamless", "elevate", "game-changing", "world-class" | Consultancy filler. Says nothing, dates fast |
| "Nollywood of Uganda", "the next ..." | Defines WUFPA by someone else |
| "Poverty", "poor", "struggling" as descriptors of members | Untrue of the audience and insulting to it |
| Exclamation marks | One per site, at most. Probably zero |

## 4. Taglines and which one to use

Two exist. Both are real. `⚠ CONFLICT` C-10, question Q2.

| Tagline | Where it lives | Character |
|---|---|---|
| **"Let the Story un Fold"** | Locked up beneath the wordmark in the official logo `[LOGO-A] [LOGO-B]` | Craft-facing. About cinema itself |
| **"Telling our stories that Transform our communities."** | Closing line of the association profile `[P3]` | Mission-facing. About purpose |

**Recommendation:**

1. **"Let the Story un Fold" stays with the logo.** It is part of the mark. It is not a
   headline and should never be typeset separately as a page heading.
2. **"Telling our stories that transform our communities" is the organisational strapline**
   — used in the footer, in metadata, in Open Graph descriptions, and anywhere WUFPA's
   purpose is stated in one line. It is the stronger line for the site's actual job, because
   it contains the mission's two halves: stories and transformation.
3. They do not compete because they never appear in the same role.

> ⚠ **The "un Fold" question.** The logo reads *"Let the Story un Fold"* — three words where
> standard English has two ("unfold"). This must be raised with WUFPA before launch, with
> the decision recorded here:
> - If it is **intentional**, keep it exactly, everywhere, without correction. It is their
>   mark.
> - If it is an **error**, WUFPA may wish to correct it in the redrawn SVG — but this means
>   the website and all existing printed material would differ. That is a decision for
>   WUFPA, not for us.
> - Until answered, **reproduce it exactly as supplied.** Do not silently "fix" a client's
>   logo.
>
> Note also the profile's capitalisation, *"Telling our stories that Transform our
> communities"*, with a capital T on "Transform" `[P3]`. This documentation treats that as a
> typographic slip and recommends sentence case, but the decision is WUFPA's.

## 5. Colour system

### 5.1 Sampled from the official assets

Values below are measured from the supplied files, not estimated.

| Colour | Hex | Source | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **WUFPA Red** | `#ED1B24` | `[LOGO-A]`, 6.35% of pixels | The film-strip and wordmark. Equivalent to CMYK 0/100/100/0 |
| **WUFPA Black** | `#231F20` | `[LOGO-A]`, 6.42% of pixels | The reel and tagline. Equivalent to CMYK 0/0/0/100 — a rich, slightly warm black, *not* `#000000` |
| **White** | `#FFFFFF` | `[LOGO-A]`, 83% of pixels | The logo is designed for white. This is the brand's ground |
| **SACCO Blue** | `#2E3192` | `[LOGO-C]` | WUFM SACCO only. Not a WUFPA colour |

> `[LOGO-B]` samples at `#D12028` red rather than `#ED1B24`. This is JPEG/WhatsApp
> recompression, not a second brand red. **`[LOGO-A]` is the colour reference.**

**Three consequences follow immediately, and they overturn the prototype's entire scheme:**

1. **WUFPA's brand ground is white, not black.** The logo is 83% white. Placing it on
   near-black, as the prototype does, means either knocking the mark out (destroying the
   black reel) or floating it in a white box (an amateur tell). Light is the correct ground.
2. **There is no gold in this brand.** The prototype's `#d4af37` is invented. So is
   `#e50914`, which is Netflix's red, not WUFPA's.
3. **The red is a signal, not a surface.** At 6% of the logo it punctuates. Large red fields
   would misrepresent the mark's proportions and, at this saturation, would be genuinely
   fatiguing to read against.

### 5.2 The working palette

Semantic tokens are specified in [07_DESIGN_SYSTEM.md](07_DESIGN_SYSTEM.md). This is the
raw palette and its accessibility behaviour.

#### Red ramp

| Token | Hex | Contrast on white | Contrast on Ink 900 | Use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `red-300` | `#FF4B52` | 2.4:1 ✗ | **4.95:1 ✓** | Links and accents **on dark surfaces only** |
| `red-500` **(brand)** | `#ED1B24` | **4.39:1** ⚠ | 3.71:1 ⚠ | The logo. Large display text ≥24px/19px-bold. UI accents, rules, focus rings, active states. **Not for body text** |
| `red-700` | `#B31419` | **6.93:1 ✓** | 1.5:1 ✗ | Inline links, red body text, small red type on white |

> **This is the most important accessibility fact in the brand.** `#ED1B24` on white is
> **4.39:1** — it fails WCAG 2.2 AA for normal-size text (4.5:1). It passes for large text
> and for non-text UI components (3:1). Every red word smaller than 24px (or 19px bold)
> must use `red-700`. This is not a compromise of the brand; the brand red still owns every
> large, loud moment on the page.

#### Ink ramp *(warm neutrals derived from `#231F20`)*

| Token | Hex | On white | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| `ink-900` | `#231F20` | 16.3:1 | Brand black. Headings, primary text, dark surfaces |
| `ink-800` | `#3A3436` | 11.4:1 | Body text on white |
| `ink-700` | `#524A4C` | 8.0:1 | Secondary text |
| `ink-600` | `#6B6163` | 5.98:1 | Muted text, captions, metadata — **the lightest text permitted on white** |
| `ink-500` | `#8A8083` | 3.82:1 | Large text only, icons, strong borders. Never body text |
| `ink-400` | `#A9A1A3` | 2.5:1 | Borders, dividers, disabled states. Never text |
| `ink-300` | `#C9C3C4` | — | Borders |
| `ink-200` | `#E3DFDF` | — | Hairlines, input borders |
| `ink-100` | `#F2EFEF` | — | Section tints, card fills |
| `ink-050` | `#FAF8F8` | — | Page alternate background |
| `white` | `#FFFFFF` | — | Primary page background |

Why warm neutrals rather than pure grey: `#231F20` is warm, and a cold grey ramp beside it
reads as two unrelated colour systems. The warmth also sits better under the supplied
photography, which is dominated by daylight, red soil, grass and skin tones.

#### Support colours

| Token | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `sacco-blue` | `#2E3192` | **WUFM SACCO contexts only.** 10.7:1 on white — safe for text |
| `success` | `#1F6B3F` | Form success. 5.9:1 on white |
| `warning` | `#8A5A00` | Form warnings. 5.4:1 on white |
| `error` | `#B31419` | Form errors — deliberately the same as `red-700`, since red is already the brand's alert colour |

> **Do not introduce a second accent colour.** Two colours and a neutral ramp is the whole
> system. A brand this graphically strong does not need help; it needs restraint. If a new
> colour seems necessary, the layout is doing too much work with colour and not enough with
> space and type.

### 5.3 Colour usage rules

1. **White is the default page ground.** Sections alternate white / `ink-050` / `ink-100`
   for rhythm.
2. **`ink-900` is used for full-bleed dark sections** — sparingly, for emphasis. Good
   candidates: the footer, a single homepage statement band, photo-led sections where a dark
   ground helps the image. Never more than two per page.
3. **Red is never a large background field.** Maximum sensible red area is a button, a tag,
   a rule, or a small tinted block. If a red area would be bigger than a paragraph, it is
   too big.
4. **Never place `red-500` on `ink-900`.** 3.71:1 — it fails for text, and the vibration
   between saturated red and near-black is uncomfortable at size. Use `red-300` on dark.
5. **SACCO blue never appears outside SACCO content.** It belongs to a different registered
   entity `[LOGO-C]`.
6. **Colour is never the only carrier of meaning** (1.4.1). Error states get an icon and
   text; the active nav item gets a rule as well as a colour; required fields get a word,
   not just an asterisk in red.
7. **All body text is `ink-800`.** Not `ink-600` "for a softer look". The prototype's grey-
   on-black is exactly the failure this rule prevents.

## 6. Typography

### 6.1 Recommendation

Two variable typefaces, both open-licensed and self-hosted.

| Role | Typeface | Licence | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Display + UI** | **Archivo** (variable, incl. width axis) | SIL OFL 1.1 | A grotesque with real poster energy and a width axis. The WUFPA wordmark is a heavy, slightly rough, dimensional sans — Archivo's condensed-to-expanded range lets headlines carry that same weight and confidence without imitating the logo. It is workmanlike rather than fashionable, which matches the organisation, and it has excellent legibility at UI sizes |
| **Long-form body** | **Source Serif 4** (variable) | SIL OFL 1.1 | Used for article and About-page reading. A serif signals *record* rather than *marketing*, which is exactly the register WUFPA's advocacy and activity reporting needs. It also separates the site instantly from the sans-only default that every template produces |

**Why not the prototype's choices:** Cinzel is a Roman-inscription capital face derived from
Trajan-era lettering. It reads as classical antiquity or luxury goods, and it belongs to a
different continent and a different century than this organisation. It is also poor at
small sizes and has limited weights. Montserrat was loaded at four weights `[SITE]` and
used for very little.

**Why not a single family:** the site has two genuinely different reading modes — scanning
an interface, and reading 800 words about copyright advocacy. One typeface optimised for
both is a compromise in both.

**Fallback stacks** (matched metrics to limit layout shift):

```
Display/UI:  Archivo, "Segoe UI", Roboto, system-ui, -apple-system, sans-serif
Body serif:  "Source Serif 4", Georgia, "Times New Roman", serif
Mono:        ui-monospace, "Cascadia Mono", Menlo, Consolas, monospace
```

**Loading rules** (constraint C1 — metered mobile data):

- Self-host. No third-party font CDN — it is a render-blocking cross-origin request and a
  privacy exposure.
- WOFF2 only, subset to Latin + Latin Extended-A.
- Two files total. If a weight is not used, it is not shipped.
- `font-display: swap`, with fallback metrics tuned via `size-adjust` / `ascent-override`
  so the swap does not shift layout.
- `<link rel="preload">` the display face only. The serif loads normally; article text
  rendering in Georgia for 200ms is not a problem.

> `⚠ GAP` — no supplied source states a WUFPA typeface. This is a recommendation. If WUFPA
> has print templates using specific fonts, they take precedence and this section is revised.

### 6.2 Type scale

Full token table in [07_DESIGN_SYSTEM.md § 4](07_DESIGN_SYSTEM.md). Principles here:

- **A modular scale of 1.25 (major third)** at mobile, opening to 1.333 (perfect fourth) at
  desktop. The larger desktop ratio gives headlines the presence the brand needs; the
  tighter mobile ratio prevents headlines eating the screen.
- **Body text is 17px minimum**, 18px on desktop. Not 16px. The audience includes older
  office-holders reading on phones in daylight, and the site carries long-form content.
- **Measure is 60–75 characters** for serif body copy. Enforce with `max-width` in `ch`.
- **Line height:** 1.6 for body serif, 1.5 for UI sans, 1.1–1.2 for display.
- **Never set display type in all caps** except for small labels/eyebrows at ≥0.05em
  tracking. The prototype set the entire hero headline in caps `[SITE]`, which is both
  harder to read and, at that size, shouty.

## 7. Photography direction

**This is the most valuable brand asset WUFPA has, and it is entirely unused today.** More
than 70 documentary photographs are recoverable from the profile PDF
([12_MEDIA_LIBRARY.md](12_MEDIA_LIBRARY.md)).

### 7.1 The governing principle

> **Every photograph of a person on this website is a real WUFPA member, at a real WUFPA
> activity.** There is no exception, no "just for the hero", no "temporarily".

The reason is not purity. WUFPA's entire proposition is that it represents real filmmakers
in real places. A stock photograph of a stranger is a lie about the one thing the
organisation is selling — and audience A1, who will recognise the places and possibly the
people, is precisely the audience most able to detect it.

### 7.2 What the real photography is like, and how to use it

The supplied archive has a consistent, usable character. Read honestly, it is:

- **Documentary, not produced.** Available light, phone and entry-level DSLR, mixed white
  balance, occasional motion blur.
- **Collective.** Overwhelmingly group photographs — 20 to 100 people, arranged, looking at
  camera.
- **Located.** Grass, red soil, brick, plastic chairs, banners, kingdom buildings,
  conference rooms, streets.
- **Warm.** Daylight, greens, reds, skin tones, printed fabric.
- **Proud.** People holding certificates, trophies, cheques. Handshakes. Arms raised.

**Do not fight this.** Attempting to make it look like commissioned agency photography — 
heavy grading, duotones, dark vignettes, film-grain overlays as in the prototype — will
make authentic images look like cheap stock. The correct treatment is *respect*:

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Present at generous size, minimally processed | Apply duotone, sepia, heavy grade, or grain overlay |
| Correct exposure and white balance only | "Cinematic" teal-and-orange grading |
| Crop for composition and for the subject's dignity | Crop faces at awkward points to fit a fixed ratio |
| Caption with real names, places and dates | Use decorative captions or none |
| Let group photographs be wide and horizontal | Force group photographs into square or portrait crops that cut people off |
| Use `object-position` to protect faces on responsive crops | Centre-crop by default |

### 7.3 Photography by context

| Context | What to show | Sources |
|---|---|---|
| **Homepage hero** | A wide, populated, unmistakably Western Ugandan scene. Strongest candidates: the Tooro workshop at Obukama bwa Tooro `[PH-3]` (2400×1600, the highest-resolution asset supplied, and the composition has natural space at right for type), or the UCC awareness workshop crowd `[P11]` | `[PH-3] [P11]` |
| **Leadership** | The real portraits from the profile `[P4] [P5]`. Consistent crop, consistent treatment, neutral background where possible | `[P4] [P5]` |
| **Programmes / workshops** | People learning: flip charts, projectors, circles of chairs, hands raised | `[P15] [P16] [P19] [P22]` |
| **Advocacy** | The piracy march — banners, movement, a brass band, hand-written placards. This is the most photographically alive material in the archive | `[P13]` |
| **Festivals / awards** | Trophies, framed certificates, the UCC cheque presentation, the branded backdrop | `[P20] [P21]` |
| **Regions** | The sub-region workshop that actually happened there. Bunyoro `[P9] [PH-2]`, Tooro `[P10] [PH-3]`, Kigezi `[P17]`, Greater Bushenyi `[P8]`, Ankole districts `[P11]`, Ntungamo/Rukungiri `[P18]` |
| **Partners** | Joint activity — the MoU signing `[P8]`, the UNATCOM workshop `[P22]`, the Matatu Film Lab cohort `[P16]`, the UFMI visit `[P17]` — rather than a logo grid | as listed |
| **Membership** | Certificate presentations. People holding proof of belonging is exactly the emotional content this page needs | `[PH-1] [PH-4] [P5] [P7]` |
| **SACCO** | The 24 October 2025 certification `[P23]`, with the SACCO logo | `[P23] [LOGO-C]` |

### 7.4 Technical and ethical standards

- **Never upscale.** Several archive images are small (down to 226×299). A small image used
  small is fine; a small image stretched across a hero is a visible failure. Size limits per
  asset are recorded in [12_MEDIA_LIBRARY.md](12_MEDIA_LIBRARY.md).
- **Never letterbox or pillarbox** to force an aspect ratio. Crop, or choose another image.
- **Every image needs meaningful `alt`.** Not "Workshop" `[SITE]`. See
  [16_SEO_ACCESSIBILITY.md § 6](16_SEO_ACCESSIBILITY.md).
- **Consent.** Photographs of identifiable people, particularly the certificate and portrait
  images, need permission to publish (question Q4). Where a photograph includes children —
  `[PH-3]` includes a child — treat consent as mandatory, not assumed.
- **Attribution.** Photographer credit where known (question Q19).

### 7.5 When there is no photograph

Do not reach for stock and do not leave a grey box. In priority order:

1. Use a different real photograph, even if less on-the-nose.
2. Use type. A well-set pull quote or a large statistic is a legitimate visual element and
   is often stronger than a mediocre photograph.
3. Use a flat colour or `ink-100` block with the wordmark. Honest and calm.

### 8.2 Required production work

The supplied logo is **JPEG on a white background**. This is not usable for a modern
website: it cannot be placed on any non-white surface, it cannot scale, and at small sizes
the thin film-strip highlights will fill in.

**Deliverables required before build:**

1. **`wufpa-logo.svg`** — the full lockup, redrawn as vectors from `[LOGO-A]`, transparent
   background, colours exactly `#ED1B24` / `#231F20`.
2. **`wufpa-logo-mono-dark.svg`** — all `#231F20`, for single-colour contexts.
3. **`wufpa-logo-reverse.svg`** — for dark surfaces: wordmark and tagline in white, reel in
   white, film strip retaining `#ED1B24`.
4. **`wufpa-mark.svg`** — reel + strip only, no wordmark. For the favicon, app icons, and
   any space under ~120px wide.
5. **Favicon set** — `favicon.svg`, 32/180/192/512px PNG, `site.webmanifest`.
6. **`wufpa-og.png`** — 1200×630 social sharing image.

> Redraw, do not trace-and-ship. An auto-traced JPEG carries compression artefacts as bumps
> in every curve, visible at large sizes. Ask WUFPA for the original vector first (question
> Q13) — it may exist.

### 8.3 Clear space and minimum size

- **Clear space:** the height of the "W" in the wordmark, on all four sides. Nothing enters
  it — no text, no image edge, no other logo, no page border.
- **Minimum sizes:**
  - Full lockup: **140px** wide on screen. Below this, "Let the Story un Fold" is
    illegible and the strip perforations close up.
  - Wordmark + reel, no tagline: **96px** wide.
  - Mark only: **24px**.
- **Header usage:** the full lockup is too tall for a fixed header. Use the mark plus a
  separately-set "WUFPA" wordmark, or a horizontal lockup variant. Do not squash the
  vertical lockup.

### 8.4 Placement

- **Preferred ground: white or `ink-050`.** The mark is designed for it.
- **On `ink-900`:** use the reverse SVG.
- **On photographs:** only with a solid backing shape or a genuinely quiet area of the
  image, and only using the reverse version. WUFPA's own practice of watermarking activity
  photographs `[P8] [P13]` is fine for social media; on the website the logo belongs in the
  interface, not on the photographs.

### 8.5 Logo don'ts

Do not: recolour it · place `#ED1B24` on `#231F20` within it · stretch, squash or rotate it ·
add drop shadows, glows, strokes or bevels · place it on a busy photograph without backing ·
reconstruct the lockup with a different typeface · alter the spacing between mark, wordmark
and tagline · use the JPEG where the SVG exists · use it smaller than § 8.3 · substitute an
emoji for it (as the prototype does, `[SITE]`) · combine it with another logo without the
clear space · use the tagline as a standalone headline.

### 8.6 WUFM SACCO identity

WUFM SACCO is a **separate registered entity** with its own mark, its own palette
(`#2E3192` blue with `#ED1B24` red) and its own strapline, "Small Savings, Big Dreams"
`[LOGO-C]`.

Rules: the SACCO logo appears only on SACCO pages and sections · SACCO blue appears nowhere
else · the two logos may appear together on the SACCO page, separated by at least the WUFPA
clear space, with WUFPA first · SACCO content is clearly labelled as the SACCO, never
presented as a WUFPA service, because the legal and financial distinction matters.

## 9. Iconography

**Recommendation: Lucide** (ISC licence, ~1,500 icons, 24×24 grid, 2px stroke).

Why: the stroke weight sits naturally with Archivo's stems; the licence is unrestricted; the
icons are individually importable as SVG so only what is used ships (constraint C1); and the
set is neutral enough to stay out of the way of the photography.

Rules:

- **Icons support text; they never replace it.** No icon-only navigation, no icon-only
  buttons except a genuinely universal close (×) — and that gets an `aria-label`.
- **One set only.** Never mix icon families.
- **`stroke-width: 2`, `currentColor`**, sized in `em` so icons scale with text.
- **Decorative icons get `aria-hidden="true"`.** Meaningful icons get an accessible name.
- **No emoji as interface elements.** The prototype uses 🎬 as the logo, ❤ on donate
  buttons, ▶ on a link and 🎬 as a list bullet `[SITE]`. Emoji render inconsistently across
  platforms, are announced verbosely by screen readers ("movie camera"), and cannot be
  styled. Emoji in editorial content, written by a human, is a different matter and is fine.
- **Film-industry icons are permitted and encouraged** where genuinely meaningful — clapper,
  reel, projector, camera. But not as decoration on every heading.

## 10. Motion principles

Motion in this brand is **functional and quiet**. Three reasons: the prototype's animation
budget (grain loop, pulse, 0.8s 40px reveals) costs battery and CPU on the mid-range devices
this audience uses (C1); motion competes with photography, which is the actual content; and
heavy motion reads as *design* rather than *record*, which is the wrong register for an
organisation asking to be taken seriously.

| Rule | Value | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Duration, micro (hover, focus, small state) | 120–180ms | Below ~100ms feels instant; above ~200ms feels slow |
| Duration, medium (disclosure, menu, modal) | 200–280ms | |
| Duration, entrance (scroll reveal) | 320–400ms | The prototype's 800ms is twice too long |
| Easing, entering | `cubic-bezier(0.2, 0, 0, 1)` | Decelerates into place |
| Easing, exiting | `cubic-bezier(0.4, 0, 1, 1)` | Leaves quickly |
| Scroll-reveal offset | ≤ 16px translate | The prototype's 40px causes visible jump and layout instability |
| Stagger | ≤ 60ms between siblings, max 5 items | Longer sequences feel like waiting |
| Parallax | None | Costs performance, causes motion discomfort, adds nothing |
| Autoplay carousels | None | WCAG 2.2.2, and it hides content from users who never see the later slides |
| Looping ambient animation | None | The prototype's `grain` and `pulse` loops run forever, burning CPU |

**Absolute requirements:**

1. **`prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` is honoured everywhere.** Under it: no translate, no
   scale, no parallax; opacity fades of ≤100ms only, or nothing at all. This is a
   requirement (WCAG 2.3.3) and is entirely absent from the prototype (X19).
2. **Content is never hidden pending animation.** Reveal effects enhance content that is
   already in the DOM and already visible in the no-JS case (X20).
3. **Nothing animates that the user did not cause**, except a single scroll-triggered
   entrance per element, once.

## 11. Copywriting guidelines

### 11.1 Headline patterns that work for WUFPA

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| **Number + place + thing** | "300 production houses. Six sub-regions. One association." |
| **Plain declarative** | "We organise the film industry of Western Uganda." |
| **The named specific** | "From Kabale to Kagadi." |
| **Verb-first, member-facing** | "Join the association that represents your work." |
| **Evidence as headline** | "In 2022 we took copyright to the streets of Mbarara." |

### 11.2 Calls to action

- **Verb + object.** "Become a member", not "Learn more". "Send a partnership enquiry", not
  "Get in touch".
- **One primary action per page.** Everything else is secondary or tertiary.
- **The primary action across the site is "Become a Member"** (goal W1). Not "Donate" — see
  [01 § 9.2 X8](01_PROJECT_FOUNDATION.md#92-critical--must-not-ship).
- **Say what happens next.** "Send your enquiry and a coordinator will contact you" beats
  "Submit".
- **Never manufacture urgency.** No countdowns, no "limited places" unless literally true.

### 11.3 The sourcing rule for writers

Before publishing any sentence containing a fact about WUFPA, check it against
[02_ORGANISATION_PROFILE.md § 13](02_ORGANISATION_PROFILE.md#13-facts-approved-for-publication).
If it is not there, it does not ship. Write around the gap, or ask WUFPA.

This rule exists because the prototype contains eight invented film titles, four invented
member companies and a fabricated comedy section. Those were not written maliciously — they
were written to fill a layout. **A layout is never a reason to invent a fact.** If there is
nothing true to put in a section, the section is wrong.

## 12. Brand do's

1. Put real photographs of real members at the centre of every page.
2. Lead with evidence: dates, places, named partners, named people.
3. Use white as the ground and let the red punctuate.
4. Name the districts and sub-regions.
5. Treat members as professionals and colleagues.
6. Expand acronyms on first use on every page.
7. Keep motion short, quiet and reduced-motion-safe.
8. Use `red-700` for any red text below 24px.
9. Give the WUFM SACCO its own visual space and keep its blue there.
10. Date every statistic.
11. Say "we don't have that yet" rather than inventing it.
12. Design for a mid-range Android phone on metered data first.

## 13. Brand don'ts

1. Never use a stock photograph of a person.
2. Never invent a film title, a member company, a statistic or a quotation.
3. Never use gold, or Netflix red, or any colour not in § 5.2.
4. Never set the brand red as body text on white — it fails contrast.
5. Never place the logo on a dark ground without the reverse version.
6. Never substitute an emoji for the logo.
7. Never publish an individual's personal mobile number.
8. Never describe a past event in the future tense.
9. Never use "empower", "beneficiary", "vibrant", "journey" or "ecosystem" in member-facing copy.
10. Never autoplay a carousel or loop an ambient animation.
11. Never hide content until JavaScript reveals it.
12. Never present a derived number (10,500) as a counted one.
13. Never add a third typeface or a second accent colour to solve a layout problem.
14. Never silently "correct" the client's logo, tagline or name — raise it, record the decision.
