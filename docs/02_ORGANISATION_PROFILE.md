## 1. The organisation in one paragraph

The Western Uganda Film Producers Association Limited (WUFPA) is a non-profit company
limited by guarantee, registered in Uganda on 4 September 2017 and headquartered on
Mbaguta Street, Mbarara Shopping Market, Mbarara City. It unites film producers, production
companies, film groups and film guilds across the six sub-regions of Western Uganda —
Ankole (Rwizi), Kigezi, Rwenzori, Tooro, Bunyoro and Greater Bushenyi. Founded by 14
production companies from Ankole and Kigezi, it now comprises over 300 film production
houses. Its stated mission is to unite, train and support film producers and creatives in
Western Uganda to create high-quality films that address key social issues, promote
cultural heritage, and contribute to sustainable community development. `[P1] [P4]`

## 2. Why WUFPA exists

*This section synthesises the problem statement implied across the profile. It is
interpretation grounded in sourced facts, and the grounding facts are tagged.*

Uganda's film industry is concentrated in Kampala. Its institutions, its funding, its
training, its buyers and its policy conversations have historically been located there.
For a filmmaker in Kabale, Kasese, Masindi or Sheema, that concentration produced five
concrete problems:

**1. No representation.** National film strategy was made without regional filmmakers in
the room. WUFPA's stated purpose includes bringing "visibility, structure, and professional
opportunities to creative[s] outside of Kampala, ensuring inclusive growth in Uganda's film
industry" `[P3]`.

**2. No protection.** Piracy was normalised. WUFPA's first president led early campaigns in
Mbarara and surrounding areas on the principle that "infringement on one's copyright is a
crime" — described in the profile as "a bold step at a time when piracy was normalized"
`[P4]`. The association has since run public sensitisation marches under the banner
"Together Against Piracy" `[P13]`.

**3. No market.** Producing a film and selling it are different problems. WUFPA's response
was to organise the buyers as well as the makers, forming the Western Uganda Film
Distributors Group to connect producers with content buyers `[P5]`, and securing regular
airtime for members' films on regional broadcasters `[P2]`.

**4. No capital.** Film production requires money before it earns money, and emerging
filmmakers had no access to affordable credit. WUFPA's response was to establish a
member-owned savings and credit cooperative, WUFM SACCO `[P2] [P3] [P23]`.

**5. No training pipeline.** Skills were passed on informally or not at all, and film was
absent from the formal education curriculum — which is why WUFPA's advocacy agenda includes
getting film and media production into Uganda's national education curriculum, particularly
in vocational and secondary schools `[P3]`.

**The pattern is consistent and it is the key to understanding the organisation:** WUFPA's
answer to every one of these problems is *organise, then negotiate*. It does not primarily
make films or give grants. It assembles filmmakers into a body large enough and formal
enough to be taken seriously — by the regulator, by broadcasters, by embassies, by
Parliament, by banks — and then negotiates on their behalf. The 300+ membership is not a
vanity statistic; it is the source of the association's leverage.

## 6. Objectives

WUFPA's objectives appear in two places in the source: five "Core Objectives" `[P1]` and a
further six constitutional objects `[P2]`. Both are reproduced. The first set is
public-facing and belongs on the website; the second is closer to the memorandum of
association and belongs in an expandable detail block or the About page's lower section.

### 6.1 Core objectives *(website-facing)*

All five as stated in the profile `[P1]`.

| Objective | As stated |
|---|---|
| **Capacity Building** | Develop skills in screenwriting, directing, acting, cinematography, editing, and film production among members through workshops, mentorships, and hands-on training |
| **Cultural Promotion** | Use film to preserve and promote the diverse cultures, languages, and traditions of the Western Uganda sub regions |
| **Advocacy & Networking** | Represent filmmakers' interests in national and regional platforms and create partnerships with stakeholders in the creative, tourism, and development sectors |
| **Community Development** | Leverage film and performing arts to support developmental programs, civic education, and youth engagement |
| **Tourism Promotion** | Produce culturally themed films that highlight the unique landscapes, histories, and heritage of Western Uganda as a tool for promoting local and international tourism |

### 6.2 Constitutional objects *(secondary)*

All six as stated in the profile `[P2]`.

- To establish, develop and manage film production studios, film stores and cinemas for
  the better marketing of film content.
- To empower and equip performing artists, producers, directors and technical personnel
  with modern information technologies, in order to bridge the gap between the local and
  international motion picture industry.
- To establish and administer a fund to support members' welfare, and to invest in projects
  and financing for members' development activities.
- To establish cultural art libraries for the exhibition of film, event performance and
  photographs, and for social, musical, theatrical and other entertainments.
- To develop, conduct and maintain research initiatives for film programme and project
  planning; and to seek viable partners for development, training, financial support,
  investment and maintenance of group projects and programmes.
- To apply to international bodies, governments, authorities, national bodies, public
  organisations, corporations, groups or persons for grants, donations, gifts,
  subscriptions and other assistance in furtherance of the association's objects.

**Note for copywriters:** the third and fourth objects are the constitutional basis for the
WUFM SACCO and for the proposed cultural art library / archive respectively. That link is
worth making explicit on the site — it shows the SACCO is not an improvisation but a
founding intention delivered eight years later.

## 7. What WUFPA actually does

The profile lists activities `[P2]`, achievements `[P2]` and needs `[P3]` separately.
Merged and organised by function, WUFPA's work falls into six programme areas. This
grouping is the agency's synthesis and is the basis for the Programmes section of the
website — full detail in [09_PROGRAMMES_AND_EVENTS.md](09_PROGRAMMES_AND_EVENTS.md).

### 7.1 Training and capacity building

Film training workshops and master classes `[P2]`; mentorships and hands-on training
`[P1]`; annual workshops and training support received from UCC/CineArts Academy, US
Mission Uganda / Film Possible Uganda, Alliance Française, Goethe-Zentrum, Uganda Film
Network, UNATCOM (Uganda National Commission for UNESCO), and the National Producers Guild
of Uganda `[P2]`. Documented instances include master classes with Kampala Film School
`[P19]`, the Matatu Film Lab with Film Possible `[P16]`, monetisation training with MTN
YoTV `[P15]`, and a training workshop with UNATCOM for UNESCO `[P22]`.

### 7.2 Advocacy, rights and policy

Representing filmmakers' interests on national and regional platforms `[P1]`;
anti-piracy and copyright sensitisation, including the "Together Against Piracy" public
campaign conducted jointly with UCC, URSB, the Uganda Film Council, UFMI and the Media
Council of Uganda `[P13]`; participation in the Uganda Parliamentary Forum for Creative
Industries `[P12]`; engagement with URSB regional offices `[P9]`; and an active campaign to
have film and media production included in Uganda's national education curriculum,
especially in vocational and secondary schools `[P3]`.

Successfully advocating to UCC to launch a regional film competition is recorded as
WUFPA's first listed achievement `[P2]` — a useful, concrete demonstration that the
advocacy function produces results.

### 7.3 Regional organisation and sensitisation

A sustained programme of sub-region and district-level sensitisation meetings and workshops
that is, by volume, the most documented thing WUFPA does. Recorded locations include
Greater Bushenyi `[P8]`, Bunyoro `[P9]`, Tooro `[P10]`, Ibanda, Kazo, Kiruhura, Kamwengye
and Kitagwenda `[P11]`, Kigezi `[P17]`, and Ntungamo and Rukungiri `[P18]`.

This is the mechanism by which WUFPA grew from 14 companies to 300+, and it is
under-represented in the existing prototype.

### 7.4 Market access and distribution

Organising Western Uganda film distributors and librarians to buy and promote local content
`[P2]`; formation of the Western Uganda Film Distributors Group `[P5]`; a Memorandum of
Understanding with Sky TV Mbarara `[P8]`; and regular airing of members' films on regional
platforms — **TV West, Tayari West TV, GNTV, Lite TV, and YoTV (Kibanda Express Channel)**
`[P2]`.

**The WUFPA Kibanda Initiative** — a community film screening project bringing local films
to towns, schools and rural communities across Western Uganda, promoting access, education
and engagement `[P2]`. Launched under the current president `[P5]` and being scaled up
under the current vice president `[P4]`.

> *Context note for writers, not a sourced claim:* a *kibanda* is a small neighbourhood
> video hall — the traditional grassroots exhibition venue in Uganda. The name signals that
> the initiative meets audiences where they already watch, rather than expecting them to
> come to a cinema. Confirm with WUFPA before publishing any explanation of the name.

### 7.5 Finance and member welfare — WUFM SACCO

The **Western Uganda Filmmakers SACCO (WUFM SACCO)**, established to support producers'
projects `[P2]` and to provide financial support to creatives `[P5]`. Its stated intent is
to offer low-interest loans and grants to young and emerging filmmakers, and small grants
for local film projects and talent development `[P3]`. Members received the WUFM SACCO
certificate on 24 October 2025, with the Mbarara City Commercial Officer `[P23]`.

It has its own visual identity and strapline: **"Small Savings, Big Dreams"** `[LOGO-C]`.

Seed capital for the SACCO is the first item on WUFPA's list of current needs `[P3]`.

### 7.6 Festivals, competitions and international exposure

- **UCC Regional Film Competition** — advocated for by WUFPA, launched by UCC, and in its
  3rd edition as of the profile's writing `[P2] [P5]`. Documented delivery includes master
  classes with Kampala Film School and award ceremonies with trophies, framed certificates
  and a UCC prize cheque `[P19] [P20] [P21]`. `⚠ VERIFY` current edition — question Q7
- **Western Uganda Film Awards Gala** — the first was scheduled for 13 December 2025 in
  Mbarara City `[P2] [P4]`. `⚠ VERIFY` outcome — question Q6
- **International delegations** — WUFPA has led delegations to benchmark and participate in
  the **African Mashariki Film Festival** and the **Zanzibar International Film Festival
  (ZIFF)** `[P3] [P5]`, and attended the **Kigali International Content Market /
  MashaRket** `[P14]`
- **Uganda Film Festival** — WUFPA has run awareness work on the UCC Content Development
  Support Programme and the Uganda Film Festival `[P11]`
- Supporting film submissions to national and international platforms `[P2]`

WUFPA is governed by an Executive Committee led by the President, Vice President, Treasurer
and Secretary. Alongside the Executive Committee the association has a Board of Trustees, a
Disciplinary Committee, a Supervisory Committee, and regional and district coordinators who
assist in the smooth running of association activities and ensure the bylaws are followed.
`[P5]`

Sub-regional leadership committees were introduced under the current president for
decentralised governance and efficient programme delivery `[P5]`.

## 11. Current needs and the case for support

Reproduced because it is the substance of the "Support WUFPA" page, and because a specific
ask outperforms a general appeal.

### 11.1 What WUFPA is asking for

The six current needs, as stated in the profile `[P3]`.

1. **WUFM SACCO seed capital** — to offer low-interest loans and grants to young and
   emerging filmmakers, and small grants for local film projects and talent development
2. **Production equipment** — professional video cameras, lighting kits, sound gear,
   editing software and computers
3. **Training and capacity building** — sponsorship for workshops and master classes;
   screenwriting, directing and post-production training; mentorships with national and
   international professionals
4. **Infrastructure** — office space and administrative facilities; a Regional Film
   Production Studio in Western Uganda; support to build or access community cinema halls
5. **International exposure** — exchange programmes and travel support to attend film
   festivals, labs and residencies, e.g. ZIFF and the African Mashariki Film Festival
6. **Advocacy for policy change** — support to include film and media production in
   Uganda's national education curriculum, especially in vocational and secondary schools

### 11.2 Why support WUFPA *(verbatim headings)*

The five reasons, with their headings exactly as printed `[P3]`.

1. **Empowering youth and women** — creative arts training and production activities open
   employment and income opportunities, especially for underrepresented groups in rural and
   semi-urban communities
2. **Preserving indigenous cultures** — authentic local storytelling in native languages
   ensures Western Uganda's heritage is captured, celebrated and passed on
3. **Strengthening regional creative economies** — film production and distribution create
   value chains in acting, editing, costume design, music and tourism
4. **Bridging regional gaps in national film strategy** — visibility, structure and
   professional opportunity for creatives outside Kampala
5. **Promoting education and civic awareness through film** — members' films address
   health, gender-based violence, environmental conservation and civic responsibility
